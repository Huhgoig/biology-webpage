Theory Notes: Homeostasis

What is Homeostasis?

The process of maintaining a constant internal environment within the body despite changes in the external environment. It operates primarily through negative feedback loops.

Components of a Loop

  • Stimulus: A change in the internal environment (e.g., glucose levels rise).
  • Receptor: The organ/tissue that detects the change (e.g., Islets of Langerhans).
  • Control Centre: Processes info and sends a signal (often the same as the receptor, or the brain/hypothalamus).
  • Effector: The organ that brings about a change (e.g., Liver/Muscles).
  • Response: The corrective action that reverses the stimulus, bringing conditions back to normal.

1. Blood Glucose Regulation

High Glucose: Beta cells in pancreas detect -> secrete Insulin -> Liver/muscles convert glucose to glycogen -> Glucose drops.

Low Glucose: Alpha cells in pancreas detect -> secrete Glucagon -> Liver converts glycogen to glucose -> Glucose rises.

2. Osmoregulation (Water)

Low Water (High salt): Osmoreceptors in hypothalamus detect -> Pituitary secretes MORE ADH -> Renal tubules become more permeable -> More water reabsorbed -> Concentrated urine.

3. Thermoregulation (Hot)

Hypothalamus detects -> Vasodilation (blood vessels dilate, more blood to skin surface, heat lost) -> Sweating increases (evaporation cools). Response: Temp drops.

4. Thyroxine (Metabolism)

Low thyroxine -> Pituitary secretes TSH -> Thyroid stimulated -> Secretes more thyroxine -> Metabolism increases -> High thyroxine inhibits TSH (Negative Feedback).

Exam Key Points & Terminology

  • ✔ "Detects a change" - Always use this phrase for the receptor. E.g., "The hypothalamus detects a change in blood temperature."
  • ✔ "Secretes" - Glands secrete hormones. Never use "produces" or "makes" loosely when referring to endocrine release.
  • ✔ Antagonistic - Hormones that work in opposite directions (e.g., Insulin and Glucagon).
  • ✔ Glycogen vs Glucagon - Do not confuse these. Glycogen is the stored carbohydrate; Glucagon is the hormone.
  • ✔ Vasodilation / Vasoconstriction - Refer to blood vessels in the skin, NOT capillaries (capillaries cannot constrict).
  • ✔ Permeability - When mentioning ADH, always specify that it changes the permeability of the collecting ducts/distal convoluted tubules in the kidney.

Connections (Extra Info)

Negative feedback isn't just for basic homeostasis! It connects deeply to other IEB topics:

Human Reproduction

High levels of Progesterone and Oestrogen inhibit the secretion of FSH (Follicle Stimulating Hormone) from the pituitary gland. This is a negative feedback loop that prevents new follicles from developing while pregnant or during the luteal phase.

Nervous System Integration

The Hypothalamus is the ultimate bridge. It is part of the nervous system (detecting temp/osmolarity via nerve impulses) but acts as an endocrine control centre (controlling the pituitary gland). This connects the speed of nerves with the duration of hormones.

Population Ecology

In ecology, predator-prey graphs are biological negative feedback loops! High prey populations cause predator populations to rise (stimulus/response), which then drives prey populations back down, restoring balance.
