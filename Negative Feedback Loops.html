Negative Feedback Loops

Grade 12 IEB Life Sciences | Interactive Lesson

What is Homeostasis?

Homeostasis is the process of maintaining a constant internal environment within the body, regardless of changes in the external environment. This ensures that cells function optimally.

The primary mechanism for maintaining homeostasis is the Negative Feedback Loop. In this loop, a change from the normal set point triggers a response that counteracts (or reverses) the initial change, bringing the system back to normal.

The Standard Sequence

Every negative feedback loop generally follows this sequence:

  • Stimulus: A deviation from the normal set point (e.g., blood sugar rises).
  • Receptor: Detects the change (e.g., Islets of Langerhans in pancreas).
  • Control Centre: Processes the information and dictates a response (often the brain or a gland).
  • Effector: The organ/tissue that carries out the response (e.g., liver/muscles).
  • Response: The action that restores the balance (e.g., absorbing glucose, lowering blood sugar).

IEB Specific Examples (Learn These!)

1. Blood Glucose Regulation

High Blood Glucose: Pancreas (Beta cells) secretes Insulin -> Liver/Muscles convert glucose to glycogen -> Blood glucose drops.

Low Blood Glucose: Pancreas (Alpha cells) secretes Glucagon -> Liver converts glycogen to glucose -> Blood glucose rises.

2. Osmoregulation (Water Balance)

Low Water (Dehydration): Hypothalamus detects -> Pituitary gland secretes more ADH (Antidiuretic Hormone) -> Kidneys (collecting ducts) become more permeable to water -> More water reabsorbed into blood -> Less, concentrated urine.

3. Thermoregulation (Body Temperature)

Hot Environment: Hypothalamus detects -> Vasodilation (blood vessels widen, more heat lost), Sweating increases.

Cold Environment: Hypothalamus detects -> Vasoconstriction (blood vessels narrow, heat conserved), Shivering (muscle contraction generates heat).

4. Thyroxin Regulation (Metabolic Rate)

Pituitary secretes TSH -> Thyroid secretes Thyroxin. High thyroxin inhibits TSH secretion (negative feedback). Low thyroxin stimulates TSH secretion.

5. Carbon Dioxide Regulation

High CO2 (low pH) in blood -> Medulla Oblongata detects -> Sends impulses to breathing muscles and heart -> Increased breathing and heart rate -> More CO2 exhaled.

IEB Exam Focus Areas

  • Graph Interpretation: Be prepared to look at a graph showing a fluctuating line around a "norm" (set point). You must be able to identify where hormones like insulin or glucagon are being secreted (e.g., when the line goes above the norm, insulin is secreted).
  • "Describe the mechanism": Always use the correct sequence: Stimulus -> Receptor -> Hormone/Nerve -> Effector -> Response. Do not skip steps.
  • Vasodilation vs Vasoconstriction: Remember that blood vessels move closer to or further from the skin surface is technically incorrect phrasing for IEB. State that the arterioles dilate (widen) sending more blood to the capillaries near the skin surface, OR constrict (narrow) sending less blood.
  • Type 1 vs Type 2 Diabetes: Often linked to blood glucose feedback loops. Type 1 is a lack of insulin production (autoimmune); Type 2 is insulin resistance (receptors on cells don't respond).

Connecting the Dots (Gr 10 & 11)

To deeply understand this section, connect it to your prior learning:

  • Grade 11 Excretion: Osmoregulation directly involves the Nephron. ADH makes the distal convoluted tubule and collecting duct more permeable.
  • Grade 11 Nervous System: The Hypothalamus and Medulla Oblongata are key control centres in the brain bridging the nervous and endocrine systems.
  • Grade 10 Transport Systems: Hormones (like Insulin, Glucagon, ADH) are transported in the blood plasma to reach target organs.
