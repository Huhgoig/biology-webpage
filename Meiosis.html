The Anatomy of Meiosis

What is Meiosis?

Meiosis is a specialized type of cell division that occurs in the reproductive organs (testes and ovaries in humans) to produce gametes (sperm and egg cells). It is known as a reduction division because it halves the number of chromosomes, turning a diploid (2n) parent cell into four haploid (n) daughter cells.

Meiosis I (Reduction Division)

  • Prophase I: Chromosomes condense. Homologous chromosomes pair up to form bivalents. Crossing over occurs at points called chiasmata, exchanging genetic material.
  • Metaphase I: Homologous pairs line up along the equator randomly (Random Arrangement). Spindle fibers attach to centromeres.
  • Anaphase I: Whole chromosomes from each pair are pulled to opposite poles. (The centromeres do not split yet).
  • Telophase I: Chromosomes reach poles. Two haploid nuclei form. Cytokinesis divides the cell.

Meiosis II (Equational Division)

Similar to mitosis, but occurs in haploid cells.

  • Prophase II: Spindle fibers reform in both cells.
  • Metaphase II: Individual chromosomes line up single file on the equator.
  • Anaphase II: Centromeres split. Chromatids (now individual chromosomes) are pulled to opposite poles.
  • Telophase II: Nuclear membranes form. Cytokinesis results in 4 genetically unique haploid cells.

Why is Meiosis Crucial?

  1. Maintains Chromosome Number: Ensures gametes are haploid (n=23) so that fertilization restores the diploid number (2n=46).
  2. Genetic Variation: Achieved through Crossing Over (Prophase I) and Random Arrangement (Metaphase I).

Abnormalities: Non-disjunction

Occurs when chromosomes fail to separate correctly during Anaphase I or Anaphase II. This results in gametes with an abnormal number of chromosomes (aneuploidy).

Down Syndrome (Trisomy 21): If non-disjunction happens at chromosome pair 21, a gamete may receive two copies. Upon fertilization, the zygote gets three copies of chromosome 21 (47 chromosomes in total).

Biological Connections

Understanding how meiosis connects to other sections of the IEB syllabus will deepen your understanding and help in essay questions.

1. Connection to Genetics

Meiosis is the physical basis of Mendel's Laws.

  • Law of Segregation: During Anaphase I, homologous chromosomes separate, meaning alleles for a trait separate into different gametes.
  • Law of Independent Assortment: The random arrangement of bivalents in Metaphase I means alleles for different traits sort independently of one another.

2. Connection to Human Reproduction

Meiosis directly applies to Gametogenesis:

  • Spermatogenesis: One diploid spermatogonium undergoes meiosis to form four equal-sized, functional haploid sperm cells in the testes.
  • Oogenesis: One diploid oogonium undergoes meiosis to form one large ovum (egg) and three small polar bodies (which degenerate) in the ovaries.

3. Connection to DNA and Chromosomes

Before meiosis begins (Interphase), DNA must replicate so each chromosome consists of two identical chromatids joined by a centromere. This ensures there is enough genetic material to divide twice.

Exam Key Points & Pitfalls

Examiner's Warning!

Do not confuse Anaphase I with Anaphase II. In Anaphase I, whole chromosomes move. In Anaphase II, chromatids move.

  • Definition required: Always define homologous chromosomes as: "A pair of chromosomes, one maternal and one paternal, that have the same size, shape, and gene loci."
  • Sources of Variation: If asked for sources of variation in meiosis, state both: 1. Crossing over (Prophase I), 2. Random arrangement (Metaphase I). Add "Random fertilization" if the question asks about sexual reproduction in general.
  • Chromatid vs Chromosome: A single thread is a chromatid. Once the centromere splits in Anaphase II, it is immediately called an unreplicated chromosome.
  • Down Syndrome: Know how to identify it from a karyotype (3 chromosomes at position 21). State clearly that it is a result of non-disjunction.
  • Mitosis vs Meiosis: Be prepared to tabulate differences (e.g., Number of divisions, crossing over occurrence, genetic nature of daughters, number of daughters, chromosome number in daughters).
