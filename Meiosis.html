Meiosis: The Reduction Division

Overview & Key Formula

Meiosis is a type of cell division resulting in four haploid daughter cells, each with half the number of chromosomes of the parent cell. It occurs in the sex organs (testes and ovaries) to produce gametes.

Formula: Diploid to Haploid 2n → n

Meiosis I (Reduction Division)

  • Prophase I: Chromosomes condense. Homologous chromosomes pair up to form bivalents. Crossing over occurs at chiasmata, exchanging genetic material.
  • Metaphase I: Bivalents align randomly at the equator (Random Arrangement).
  • Anaphase I: Homologous chromosomes separate and move to opposite poles.
  • Telophase I: Two haploid cells form, though chromosomes are still composed of two chromatids.

Meiosis II (Equational Division)

Similar to mitosis, but occurs in haploid cells.

  • Prophase II: Spindle fibers form in both cells.
  • Metaphase II: Individual chromosomes align at the equator.
  • Anaphase II: Centromeres split; chromatids are pulled to opposite poles.
  • Telophase II: Four genetically unique haploid gametes are formed.

Non-disjunction

Failure of homologous chromosomes (Anaphase I) or sister chromatids (Anaphase II) to separate properly. This leads to aneuploidy (e.g., Trisomy 21 / Down Syndrome, where a gamete has an extra chromosome 21).

Connections to Other Grade 12 Sections

🔗 Genetics (Inheritance)

Meiosis explains Mendel's laws. Law of Segregation: Homologous chromosomes separate in Anaphase I. Law of Independent Assortment: Random arrangement of bivalents in Metaphase I creates independent allele combinations.

🔗 DNA & Replication

Before meiosis can even begin, DNA replication must occur during Interphase. This ensures each chromosome consists of two identical sister chromatids before they enter Prophase I.

🔗 Human Reproduction

Meiosis is the mechanism of gametogenesis. In males (spermatogenesis), meiosis produces four functional sperm. In females (oogenesis), it produces one functional ovum and three polar bodies. Fertilization restores the diploid number (n + n = 2n).

IEB Exam Key Points

🚨 High Yield Topics

  • Sources of Variation: You must list exactly three: 1. Crossing over (Prophase I), 2. Random arrangement (Metaphase I/II), 3. Random fertilization (after meiosis).
  • Down Syndrome: Understand it is caused by non-disjunction at Chromosome 21, resulting in a somatic cell with 47 chromosomes (45 autosomes + 2 gonosomes).
  • Mitosis vs Meiosis: Be prepared to tabulate differences (e.g., 1 division vs 2 divisions, somatic cells vs sex organs, identical cells vs genetic variation).
  • Identifying Phases from Diagrams: If chromosomes are in pairs (bivalents), it's Meiosis I. If single chromosomes are involved and crossing over has clearly occurred (mixed colors), it's Meiosis II.
