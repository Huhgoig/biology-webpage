Anatomy of the Human Ear

Grade 12 Life Sciences (IEB) Interactive Lesson

Structure and Function of the Ear

The human ear is divided into three main sections: the outer ear, the middle ear, and the inner ear. It is responsible for two distinct functions: hearing and balance.

1. The Outer Ear

  • Pinna (Auricle): Funnel-shaped cartilaginous structure. Collects and directs sound waves into the auditory canal.
  • Auditory Meatus (Ear Canal): Tube leading to the eardrum. Contains ceruminous glands that secrete wax (cerumen) to trap dust and prevent infection.

2. The Middle Ear

An air-filled cavity within the temporal bone.

  • Tympanic Membrane (Eardrum): A thin membrane that vibrates when sound waves strike it, transferring mechanical energy to the ossicles.
  • Ossicles: Three smallest bones in the body: Malleus (Hammer), Incus (Anvil), and Stapes (Stirrup). They form a lever system that amplifies sound vibrations and transmits them to the oval window.
  • Eustachian Tube: Connects the middle ear to the pharynx (throat). Equalizes air pressure on both sides of the tympanic membrane to prevent it from bursting.
  • Oval Window: Membrane covered by the stapes. Transmits vibrations into the fluid of the inner ear.
  • Round Window: Membrane that bulges outward to absorb and dissipate pressure waves in the cochlear fluid, preventing an echo.

3. The Inner Ear

A complex fluid-filled system called the bony and membranous labyrinths (perilymph and endolymph).

  • Cochlea: Snail-shaped structure dedicated to hearing. Contains the Organ of Corti (the receptor for hearing) equipped with hair cells.
  • Semicircular Canals: Three fluid-filled tubes arranged at right angles. Contain receptors called cristae at their base (ampullae) responsible for detecting dynamic balance (sudden movements/rotation).
  • Sacculus and Utriculus (Vestibule): Contain receptors called maculae responsible for static balance (position of head relative to gravity).

Mechanism of Hearing

Pinna traps sound waves → Travels down auditory canal → Tympanic membrane vibrates → Ossicles amplify and transmit vibrations → Stapes vibrates against oval window → Pressure waves generated in perilymph/endolymph of cochlea → Hair cells of Organ of Corti are stimulated (mechanical to electrical conversion) → Nerve impulse generated → Auditory nerve transmits to cerebrum (temporal lobe) for interpretation.

Exam Focus: Key Points to Memorize

  • Amplification of Sound: Achieved by (1) the lever action of the ossicles and (2) the large surface area of the tympanic membrane compared to the small surface area of the oval window.
  • Pressure Equalization: Always state that the Eustachian tube equalizes pressure on both sides of the tympanic membrane.
  • Stimulus Conversion: The ear acts as a transducer. It converts mechanical energy (vibrations) into electrical energy (nerve impulses).
  • Receptor Names (Vital for IEB):
    • Hearing: Organ of Corti (in cochlea).
    • Dynamic Balance: Cristae (in ampullae of semicircular canals).
    • Static Balance: Maculae (in sacculus and utriculus).
  • Role of Round Window: It is highly tested. It bulges out to relieve pressure in the inner ear fluid, allowing vibrations to continue through the cochlea without reverberation.

Cross-Sectional Biology Connections

In IEB exams, topics are often integrated. Use these links to build holistic understanding:

  • The Nervous System: The ear is a sense organ packed with mechanoreceptors. Once stimulated, a sensory neuron carries the impulse via the Auditory Nerve (Cranial Nerve VIII).
  • The Brain:
    • Impulses for hearing go to the Temporal Lobe of the Cerebrum for interpretation.
    • Impulses for balance go to the Cerebellum, which coordinates muscle movements to maintain posture.
  • Homeostasis & Proprioception: Balance is part of maintaining a stable internal state in relation to gravity. The ear works with proprioceptors in muscles and joints, and the eyes, to keep the body upright.
  • Tissue Types: The pinna is elastic cartilage, ossicles are bone tissue, and the tympanic membrane relies on thin epithelial and connective tissue.
