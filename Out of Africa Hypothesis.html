The Out of Africa Hypothesis

Grade 11 IEB Life Sciences | Human Evolution & Genetics Module

IEB Curriculum Aligned

1. Executive Summary & Core Concept

The Out of Africa Hypothesis (also known as the Recent Single-Origin Hypothesis or Replacement Model) states that modern Homo sapiens evolved exclusively in Africa approximately 200 000 to 300 000 years ago. A small subgroup migrated out of Africa roughly 60 000 to 70 000 years ago, spreading across Europe, Asia, and eventually the Americas, completely replacing older hominin populations (such as Homo erectus and Homo neanderthalensis) with minimal to localized interbreeding.

Out of Africa vs. Multiregional Hypothesis

Multiregional Hypothesis: Proposes that Homo erectus left Africa ~2 million years ago and evolved simultaneously into modern Homo sapiens across different global regions (Africa, Europe, Asia), maintained by continuous gene flow between populations.

Out of Africa Hypothesis (IEB Favoured): Proposes a single, recent origin in Africa, followed by migration and replacement. Supported overwhelmingly by genetic data.

2. Lines of Genetic Evidence

The IEB syllabus places significant emphasis on the genetic mechanisms used to trace maternal and paternal lineages back to Africa.

A. Mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA) — Mitochondrial Eve

  • Location & Inheritance: mtDNA is located inside the mitochondria in the cytoplasm. During fertilization, only the sperm nucleus enters the ovum; the sperm's mitochondria (located in the neck/tail) are degraded. Therefore, mtDNA is inherited exclusively down the maternal line (mother to child).
  • Lack of Recombination: Unlike nuclear DNA, mtDNA does not undergo crossing over during meiosis. It remains unchanged from generation to generation except for random mutations.
  • Molecular Clock Concept: Mutations in mtDNA accumulate at a relatively constant rate over thousands of years. By calculating the number of differences in mtDNA sequences between modern global populations, geneticists can estimate when populations diverged.
  • Key Finding: African populations exhibit the highest level of mtDNA genetic diversity. Because mutations accumulate over time, the population with the greatest diversity must be the oldest. Modern human mtDNA traces back to a single ancestral female who lived in Africa roughly 150 000–200 000 years ago ("Mitochondrial Eve").

B. Y-Chromosome DNA — Y-Chromosomal Adam

  • Inheritance: The non-recombining region of the Y-chromosome (NRY) is passed exclusively from father to son.
  • Traceability: Like mtDNA, Y-DNA mutates predictably without paternal-maternal genetic shuffling.
  • Key Finding: Genetic tracing of the Y-chromosome leads back to a male ancestor ("Y-Chromosomal Adam") who lived in Africa around the same time period. Global populations outside Africa contain only a subset of African Y-chromosome lineages.
The Founder Effect & Genetic Bottlenecks

As small groups of Homo sapiens left Africa, they carried only a small fraction of the total genetic variation present in the parent African population. This serial Founder Effect explains why indigenous African populations today have far higher genetic diversity than European, Asian, or Native American populations.

3. Fossil & Archaeological Evidence

South Africa and East Africa host the richest hominin fossil record on Earth, providing chronological proof of human origin.

Key African Fossil Sites to Know for IEB:

  • Cradle of Humankind (Gauteng, South Africa): Includes Sterkfontein Caves (Australopithecus africanus - Mrs Ples, Little Foot), Rising Star Cave (Homo naledi), and Swartkrans (fire usage).
  • Klasies River Caves & Blombos Cave (Eastern/Western Cape, SA): Evidence of early modern Homo sapiens (~100 000 yrs ago) demonstrating complex behavior (symbolic ochre engravings, shell beads, refined stone tools).
  • East African Rift Valley (Ethiopia, Kenya, Tanzania): Olduvai Gorge, Hadar (Lucy - A. afarensis), and Omo Kibish (early modern human remains).

4. Mathematical & Genetic Principles in Human Evolution

IEB questions often test your ability to work with rates of mutation and phylogenetic trees.

The Molecular Clock Formula

Time of Divergence (T) = Number of Sequence Differences (D) x Mutation Rate (mutation rate per million years)

Note: We multiply mutation rate by 2 because both lineages accumulate mutations independently after splitting from the common ancestor.

IEB Exam High-Yield Summary

If you get an essay or structured question on the Out of Africa hypothesis, ensure you include these core bullet points to score maximum marks.

1. Definition & Timeline

  • Homo sapiens originated in Africa 200k–300k years ago.
  • Migrated out of Africa ~60k–70k years ago.
  • Replaced existing hominins worldwide.

2. Genetic Evidence (mtDNA)

  • Inherited purely via maternal line (no recombination).
  • Highest genetic diversity found in indigenous African populations.
  • Highest diversity = oldest population = point of origin.

3. Genetic Evidence (Y-DNA)

  • Passed directly from father to son via non-recombining region.
  • Traces back to male ancestors ("Y-Chromosomal Adam") in Africa.

4. Fossil Evidence

  • Oldest fossils of Homo sapiens are found in Africa (e.g., Jebel Irhoud, Florisbad, Omo).
  • Fossil transition from Australopithecus → early Homo → modern Homo sapiens is only complete in Africa.

5. Cultural/Archaeological

  • Blombos Cave (SA): Ochre carvings show early symbolic thought (~75k yrs).
  • Klasies River (SA): Bone tools, hearths, advanced Middle Stone Age blade technology.

6. Key Anatomical Changes

  • Bipedalism adaptations (foramen magnum position, S-shaped spine, bowl-shaped pelvis).
  • Enlarged cranial capacity (~1400 cc).
  • Reduced brow ridges, flatter face, and distinct chin.

Synoptic Connections across Grade 11 & 12 Life Sciences

IEB exams heavily reward students who connect concepts across different topics. Here is how Human Evolution links to other units in your curriculum:

Link 1: Meiosis & Inheritance vs. Non-Recombining DNA

Standard Inheritance: During Meiosis I (Prophase I), crossing over occurs between homologous chromosomes, shuffling maternal and paternal alleles. Random assortment adds to variation.

Why mtDNA and Y-DNA are Special: mtDNA doesn't undergo meiosis or crossing over. It stays intact generation after generation, changing ONLY via spontaneous mutation. This makes it a clean, un-shuffled historical ledger.

Link 2: Natural Selection & Environmental Shifts

The transition from forest-dwelling apes to bipedal hominins in East/South Africa was driven by climate change during the Pliocene epoch. As forests shrank into open savannahs, selection favoured individuals who could walk upright (energetically efficient over long distances, reduced solar heat exposure, hands freed for tools).

Link 3: Population Genetics & Speciation

When small groups migrated out of Africa, geographic isolation (rivers, deserts, mountain ranges) and different environmental pressures led to localized adaptation (e.g., lighter skin pigmentation in northern latitudes to maximize Vitamin D synthesis under low UV radiation). This is a textbook example of Allopatric Speciation mechanisms operating on sub-populations.
